Nifty Condition Desk methodology

Educational research only. Not investment advice. The author is not a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser or Research Analyst. Past patterns do not guarantee anything. No derivatives (F&O) and no individual stocks.

Built on the same code, data rules and learning loop as STTIE, Gold Desk and LGD Desk, but deliberately without any action call.

1. What it is

  • Audience: Gujarati-speaking salaried people and small business owners who want to understand where the Indian market stands.
  • What it shows: a market condition read: where valuation, trend and volatility sit against their own history, the economic backdrop, and what similar conditions were followed by in the past. Plus a scored forecast log kept as a public research record.
  • What it never shows: any action call, price level to aim for, loss limit, position size or allocation, derivatives, or single stocks.

2. Principles

  • No invented data. Every number is fetched from a named public source with its date, or is arithmetic on those numbers with the formula shown. Where a source is missing, the tile stays grey.
  • No AI produces any number or text. All wording is fixed templates. Headlines are context only, and headlines that themselves carry trading calls are filtered out.
  • Zero running cost: static GitHub Pages and a GitHub Actions job at 08:30, 14:30 and 20:30 IST on weekdays. No keys, no backend, no login.

3. Data

  • Nifty 50 close, P/E, P/B, dividend yield (daily since 1999) and India VIX (public history from 2018): NSE Indices Ltd, niftyindices.com historical data report. Official.
  • P/E basis change: from 31 Mar 2021 NSE Indices computes P/E on consolidated earnings (before: standalone); P/E fell from 40.4 to 33.2 overnight. So P/E is compared only with its history since then; the long valuation history uses P/B, whose basis did not change.
  • RBI repo rate and USD/INR (FBIL reference rate): RBI homepage. USD/INR history: ECB via Frankfurter.
  • CPI inflation: MoSPI CPI API (all-India, combined, base 2024).
  • India 10-year G-sec yield (monthly, OECD series from RBI data), US 10-year yield, Brent: FRED.
  • FII/DII net flows: NSE provisional cash-market data, one day at a time; history accumulates from each refresh.
  • Headlines: Google News RSS.

4. Condition read

  • Valuation band: percentile of today's P/B among all daily values since 1999: below 20 = cheap vs its history, 20–40 below average, 40–60 around average, 60–80 above average, above 80 expensive vs its history. P/E percentile since 31 Mar 2021 is shown alongside.
  • Trend state: rising = close above the 200-day average and 50-day average above 200-day; falling = both below; otherwise mixed.
  • Volatility: India VIX percentile since 2018: bottom third calm, middle normal, top third nervous.
  • Earnings-yield gap = 100 ÷ P/E − India 10-year G-sec yield (percentage points); percentile since 31 Mar 2021.
  • What this has historically meant: the median, 10th–90th percentile range and share of rises of Nifty's later price change (1, 3 or 12 months) on past days in the same state, next to the same figures for all days. These windows overlap, so independent examples are far fewer than the day counts, and the band cut-offs use the whole sample (hindsight). Description, not prediction.

5. Research forecast log and learning

  • Horizons: 21 trading days (about 1 month) and 63 (about 3 months). Forecast of the Nifty log change = drift + weights × (momentum, % above the 200-day average, P/B vs its 5-year median, 20-day realised volatility vs its 1-year median, USD/INR change).
  • Baselines: drift (average change over all earlier history, the honest baseline for equities) and 'no change'.
  • Weights are re-fitted every run by ridge least squares. Walk-forward back-test from 2008, every 5th trading day, each forecast fitted only on data available before it. A variable keeps weight only if it alone cuts walk-forward error by at least 1% versus drift, and the kept set must beat both drift and 'no change'. Otherwise all weights are zero and the forecast equals the drift.
  • Every trading day the log records both forecasts with the model version, scores them when the horizon passes (error and band right or wrong, with drift and 'no change' scored the same way), and keeps every miss. data/track_record.csv, data/models/ and the changelog keep the history.
